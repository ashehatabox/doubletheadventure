## Story in short
A small, shy rabbit in a quiet forest loves to help, but never wants credit. When a dry spell empties the watering hole, he slips out at night and mends a broken pipe. When wind knocks down a mother bird's nest and an egg goes missing, he finds it in the dark and brings it back. When snow buries the paths to the animals' food, he clears them while everyone sleeps. Each morning the animals wonder who helped, and Bunny stays hidden. A squirrel finally catches him and asks why he keeps it secret; Bunny says helping is about brightening someone's day, not being noticed. Talk of a mysterious "Secret Hero" spreads, and others start helping too: a monkey clears water paths and the birds sing to cheer everyone. Soon the forest is full of quiet good deeds, and small efforts add up to a big difference.

- **Mama Bird** (bird, species not named): loses her nest and an egg to a strong wind; Bunny brings the egg back.
- **The monkey**: inspired by the Secret Hero, starts helping by clearing water paths.

## The real animals
- Rabbit (species not named; the cover art should settle it: eastern cottontail, *Sylvilagus floridanus*, in North America, or European rabbit, *Oryctolagus cuniculus* (check)): eastern cottontails live in fields, meadows and woodland edges in eastern and central North America; European rabbits are native to Spain, Portugal and southwest France and live in burrow systems (warrens) elsewhere where they were introduced. Rabbits are mostly active at dawn, dusk and at night (crepuscular), which fits Bunny's night-time helping.
- Squirrel (species not named; red squirrel or eastern grey squirrel (check)): tree squirrels store nuts and seeds for winter and rely on finding them under snow, which links to the snowy-paths scene.
- Bird (species not named (check)): many forest birds build cup nests in trees; eggs need warmth to hatch.
- Monkey (species not named (check)): monkeys do not usually share a snowy forest with rabbits and squirrels. The one real exception writers could mention is the Japanese macaque (*Macaca fuscata*), which lives in snowy mountain forests in Japan (check before using).

## Setting and environment
A peaceful forest across the seasons: a hot, dry spell when the watering hole dries up, a windy day, and a snowy winter when the paths are buried. Landmarks: the watering hole and a pipe that feeds it, Mama Bird's nest in a tree, the forest paths, tall grass where Bunny hides. Most of Bunny's helping happens at night.

## The value
For a child: being kind means helping because it makes someone's day better, even if nobody ever knows it was you.
For a grown-up: the book separates kindness from praise. Bunny helps quietly, and the good he does spreads because others are inspired by it, not because he is rewarded. It shows that small, ordinary acts (fixing, finding, clearing, singing) count.
What the book avoids: kindness as a way to be liked or thanked; the idea that only big deeds matter. Writers should not imply that saying thank you or being seen is wrong; Bunny's quietness is his choice, not a rule.

## Where the value comes from
Shared across traditions. Related sayings:
- **Ancient Greece & Rome**: in Aesop's fable the blustering North Wind cannot tear off a traveller's cloak, but the Sun's gentle warmth makes him take it off; "Kindness effects more than severity." Source: Aesop, The North Wind and the Sun (Perry Index 46; Avianus 4), moral as worded by J. Jacobs (1894). care_flag: none.
- **Korea**: kind Heungbu chases off a snake and mends a swallow's broken leg, and its gift seed grows gourds full of treasure; his greedy brother Nolbu copies him for gain and is punished. ## Key moments
1. We meet Bunny, small and shy, who loves helping others.
2. A dry spell empties the watering hole; the animals panic. That night Bunny repairs a broken pipe, and by morning the water is back. Nobody knows who did it.
3. Wind blows Mama Bird's nest down and an egg goes missing. Bunny searches in the dark, finds it and carries it home, then slips away unseen.
4. Winter snow covers the paths to the animals' food. Bunny clears them overnight; again the animals wonder who helped.
5. A squirrel spots Bunny and asks why he hides it. Bunny explains that the point is making someone's day better, not being noticed.
6. Rumours of a "Secret Hero" spread through the forest.
7. Others join in: the monkey clears water paths and the birds sing to cheer everyone.
8. The forest becomes a place full of hidden kindnesses, where small efforts make a big difference.

## Refrain and theme line
- Refrain: none as a fixed line. The book repeats a pattern: after each problem the animals ask who helped them ("Who did this?" and similar), and sound words mark Bunny's night work (tap-tap, scrape-scrape).
- Theme line: "It's not about being noticed. It's about making someone's day better."

## Questions to ask
1. Why do you think Bunny liked to help in secret?
2. How do you think the animals felt when they woke up and found the problem fixed?
3. Why did the monkey and the birds start helping too?
4. Is there a small, quiet thing you could do to make someone's day better tomorrow?
5. How does it feel inside when you help someone, even if nobody sees?

## Notes
- The theme line is spoken by Bunny near the end; the book's last page is narration about small efforts making a big difference (paraphrase that, don't quote it).
- Bunny's night outings are story logic. In activity or tips sections, keep children's helping with a grown-up and in daylight; don't suggest kids fix pipes or go out alone at night.
- Real-life note: if a child finds a bird's egg or a fallen nest, the safe advice is to tell a grown-up and not handle it (check local wildlife guidance before giving specifics).
- The dedication honours "those who do good quietly". Authors: Amr Mohamed and Abdallah Shehata; illustration by Ahmed Beca.
