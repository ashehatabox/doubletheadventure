## Story in short
Finn, a young fish still learning to swim in the currents, adores his big sister Coral, who is fast, strong and knows every hiding spot on the reef. One morning she promises to stay close while they explore somewhere new, but in her excitement she races ahead and leaves him behind. Alone, Finn is frightened when a big shadow passes over: it's a curious dolphin, who kindly tells him to stay calm because his sister will come back. When Coral returns, Finn tells her he doesn't trust her any more, and she feels terrible. For days he keeps his distance. Then their mother sends them to collect shells at the reef's edge. Coral invites Finn and promises to stay by his side. Day after day she keeps her word, and slowly Finn trusts her again. They learn that trust means showing up for each other.

## Characters
- **Finn** (young fish, he): admires his sister; still learning to swim in currents; feels scared and let down, then slowly rebuilds trust.
- **Coral** (fish, big sister): strong, fast, knows the reef; gets carried away and breaks her promise, then makes it right by keeping it day after day. Spelled "Carol" in a few places in the book (typo; use Coral).
- **The dolphin**: curious and kind; reassures Finn while he's alone.
- **Their mother** (fish): sends them on the seashell errand that gives them a second chance.

## The real animals
- Finn and Coral (reef fish, species not named (check illustrations); if they are clownfish, *Amphiprion* species, they live in warm Indian and Pacific Ocean reefs, sheltering among sea anemones). Many small reef fish do stay close to shelter because open water is risky, which fits the "stick with me" idea.
- Dolphin (species not named; likely common bottlenose dolphin, *Tursiops truncatus* (check)): found in warm and temperate seas worldwide, including around reefs. Dolphins are mammals: they breathe air through a blowhole, live in groups called pods and communicate with clicks and whistles. Note: dolphins eat fish, so a dolphin comforting a small fish is a gentle story bend.
- Coral reef: built by tiny animals (coral polyps) over many years; home to a huge share of ocean life; threatened by warming seas (coral bleaching) and pollution (check figures before using).

## Setting and environment
A shimmering blue sea and a coral reef full of hiding spots, on a sunny morning, then several days later at the reef's edge. Ocean currents, a passing shadow in open water, seashells near the edge of the reef.

## The value
For a child: when we promise something, people count on us. If we break a promise, we can earn trust back by keeping our word again and again.
For a grown-up: the book shows that trust is built by actions over time, not by a single "sorry" or a new promise. Finn is allowed to be hurt and wary; Coral repairs things by consistently showing up. It also touches on the responsibility of older siblings.
What the book avoids: forcing the hurt child to trust again immediately; making Coral a bad sister (she got excited and made a mistake).

## Where the value comes from
Shared across traditions. Related sayings:
- **Ancient Greece & Rome**: when a bear appears, one traveller climbs a tree and leaves his friend alone; afterwards the friend says, "Never trust a friend who deserts you at a pinch." Source: Aesop, The Travellers and the Bear (Perry Index 65; Avianus), moral as worded by J. Jacobs (1894). care_flag: none.
- **Ancient China**: a saying of Chu: getting a hundred jin of gold is not as good as getting one promise from Ji Bu, a man who always kept his word. Original idiom: 一諾千金. Source: Shiji (Records of the Grand Historian) ch. 100, Biography of Ji Bu and Luan Bu. care_flag: none.
- **Korea**: the third of the five precepts for secular life, "trust among friends": keep friendships with faithfulness and sincerity. Original: 교우이신 (交友以信). Source: Samguk sagi vol. 45, Biography of Gwisan (precepts given by the monk Won'gwang, c. 600). care_flag: none.

## Notes
- Moment articles: being left behind or lost is a real fear for young children. Keep tips practical (stay where you are, find a helper such as a parent with children or a staff member) and check any safety advice against reputable child-safety sources.
- Real reef fish don't usually live in families with a mother who sends them on errands, and fish don't collect seashells; both are story bends.
- Ocean and reef care is a natural environment angle (reef-safe habits, not touching coral). Check any claims about sunscreen and reefs before using.
