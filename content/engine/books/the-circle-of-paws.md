## Story in short
Killa, a little chinchilla high in the Andes, is the fastest helper on a mountain where everybody helps everybody. She carries seeds and grass for her neighbours in her striped bag, but whenever anyone offers to help her, she laughs and says she can do it herself. Her best friend Sami the vicuña keeps offering and keeps being turned down. When a hailstorm knocks down her wall and fills her burrow with ice, she refuses help again and struggles all night. At sunrise Sami gently compares helping to a game of catch: you can't only throw. Asking feels harder than lifting any stone, but Killa asks, and everyone pitches in. Being helped feels strange at first, then warm, and her helpers thank her for letting them help. When the biggest snow buries burrows all over the mountain, Killa goes from burrow to burrow asking who needs help and who can help. A tiny hummingbird thinks she's too small to give, so she can't ask; Killa shows her that her gift is seeing from the sky. Together they dig out the whole mountain.

## Characters
- **Killa** (chinchilla): silver-grey fur, very big round ears, long whiskers, bushy tail, a striped woven bag in red, orange and pink. Generous, independent to a fault.
- **Sami** (vicuña): golden-cinnamon wool, white chest, long neck, a small red woven tassel on his left ear. Gentle; never angry.
- **Kinti** (Andean hillstar hummingbird): olive-grey back, shimmering green-and-violet throat; roosts in a rock nook.
- **Viscachas:** rabbit-like ears, long curly tail; one young one has a black stripe down its back.
- **Guinea pigs:** a family of wild cavies.

## The real animals
- **Chinchillas** (*Chinchilla*): rodents of the high Andes with extremely dense fur; take dust baths; wild populations are endangered after historic fur hunting. (Check which species/region.)
- **Vicuña** (*Vicugna vicugna*): wild relative of the alpaca living in the high puna of Peru, Bolivia, Argentina and Chile; its wool is among the finest in the world; protected after near-extinction. Wild vicuñas are not shorn by individuals; communities run regulated round-ups (*chaccu*) (check).
- **Mountain viscacha** (*Lagidium*): chinchilla relatives living among rocks.
- **Wild guinea pig / cavy** (*Cavia*): ancestor of the domestic guinea pig, native to South America.
- **Andean hillstar** (*Oreotrochilus estella*): high-altitude hummingbird that can go into torpor and roost in rock crevices on freezing nights.

## Setting and environment
The high Andes (puna) above old stone terraces: golden ichu grass, rocky slopes, snowy peaks, a twisted queñua (Polylepis) tree. From the end of harvest through a hailstorm and deep winter snow to the first spring flowers.

## The value
For a child: help is a circle; asking for help is brave, and letting others help you is a gift to them. For grown-ups: reciprocity, not keeping score: receiving is half of giving; independence taken too far shuts friends out; everyone, even the smallest, has something to give. Avoids: counting favours, shaming the helper, the lonely child fixing it alone, and a frightening storm.

## Where the value comes from
**Ayni** (Quechua and Aymara, Andes of Peru and Bolivia): reciprocity, "today I help you, tomorrow you help me"; families still take turns working each other's fields and building houses. ## Key moments
1. On Killa's mountain everyone helps everyone.
2. Killa helps first and fastest but never accepts help.
3. Sami offers; his ears droop when she races off.
4. Hailstorm wrecks her burrow; she refuses help again.
5. Turning point: Sami's "game of catch"; Killa asks for help.
6. Everyone helps, paw to paw.
7. A cosy new burrow; the helpers thank her for letting them.
8. The biggest snow; she asks who needs help and who can help.
9. Kinti thinks she's too small; Killa shows her gift is seeing from the sky.
10. Climax: the whole mountain dug out together.
11. Spring: Killa says "Yes, please!" too.

## Refrain and theme line
- Refrain: "Paw to paw, round and round!"
- Theme line: "Help is a circle. Let it come back around."
