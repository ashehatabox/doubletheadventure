## Story in short
Zayn, a young weaver bird in the misty green mountains of southern Oman, has the fastest beak in the colony and is always finished first. When the weavers warn that the big wind is coming and every nest must be ready, he weaves a quick nest for himself and his little sister Rima by lunchtime, and Hana the chameleon asks whether it's done, or done well. That evening drizzle makes it sag; he patches it and calls it good enough. The next day the wind drops it into the pool below, and he realises fast isn't the same as good. Watching Hana grip, test and then step, he decides to do it well this time, and weaving slowly feels good. But then he spots a crooked knot, unties it, and another, and another; days pass, the others are finished, and Rima waits shivering as grey clouds roll in. Hana's remark that she'd never get anywhere waiting for a perfect branch helps him see his nest is strong and good, and he finishes it just as the first big gust arrives. All night the nest swings and every knot holds. Afterwards, he teaches the young weavers.

## Characters
- **Zayn** (Rüppell's weaver): bright yellow body, chestnut-brown face mask, olive wings, short pointed dark beak, a tiny woven grass ring on his left leg. Quick; learns to balance quality and finishing.
- **Rima** (his little sister): fluffy pale-yellow chick with a brown cap of feathers; the one who needs the work done.
- **Hana** (Arabian chameleon): leaf-green with yellow bands, curly tail, swivelling cone eyes, a pale blue stripe along her side; careful but always takes the step.
- **The colony:** yellow weavers with round nests hanging over the pool; the young weavers at the end.

## The real animals
- **Rüppell's weaver** (*Ploceus galbula*): found in north-east Africa and south-west Arabia (Yemen, Saudi Arabia, Oman, including Dhofar); males weave hanging nests (check).
- **Weaver birds** knot grass with their beaks and feet; a 2011 study of southern masked weavers (Walsh et al.) found individuals drop fewer blades as they gain experience, i.e. they improve with practice (check citation).
- **Arabian chameleon** (*Chamaeleo arabicus*): lives in south-west Arabia including Dhofar; moves slowly, rocking and gripping; independently rotating eyes; colour change is mostly for signalling and temperature (check).

## Setting and environment
Dhofar, southern Oman, during the *khareef*: the summer monsoon season (roughly June to September) when mist and drizzle turn the coastal mountains green. Places: green hills by the sea, the Wadi Darbat waterfall and pool, frankincense trees on drier slopes. Arc: early drizzle, gusty days, the big wind, the fresh green morning after.

## The value
For a child: do it well, and get it done. For grown-ups: care and quality balanced with finishing: rushing makes weak work, perfectionism means it never gets done, and someone is waiting for it. Good work has room for small flaws ("crooked knots and all"). Avoids: perfectionism as a virtue, shaming speed, working for praise, the friend fixing it, a scary storm.

## Where the value comes from
**Itqan** (Arabic): doing your work with care and thoroughness, all the way to the end (root t-q-n, "to master"). It shows in the region's crafts, such as Omani palm-leaf weaving, made strand by strand until finished and ready to use.
