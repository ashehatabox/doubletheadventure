## Story in short
Langa is the tallest young giraffe on a golden Southern African plain, and from up high everything looks fine to him. He doesn't notice that his galloping kicks dust into the face of his friend Themba the meerkat, that he eats the only pods the small animals could reach, or that nobody can keep up when he leads the game. When his friends droop, Themba tells him he simply can't see it from up there. That night Langa decides he wants to understand: he does the awkward, wobbly giraffe bow, front legs spread wide and neck lowered right down, and asks Themba to show him. At meerkat height he discovers the grass is a forest, dust stings, and the waterhole bank is too steep for a tortoise. He listens, then helps: he presses steps into the mud, bends branches down, and walks softly. Later, he notices Themba silently straining on tiptoe to see the sunset and lifts him up between his horns, so each friend sees the other's view. The first rain turns the plain green.

## Characters
- **Langa** (giraffe): honey-gold coat with chestnut patches, a heart-shaped patch on the left of his neck, long lashes, two small furry ossicones. Kind but unaware.
- **Themba** (meerkat): sandy coat, dark eye rings, stands tall, wears a little woven grass sun hat. Leads Langa into the small world.
- **Kulu** (leopard tortoise): spotted cream shell with a green acacia leaf on top; can't get down the steep bank.
- **Guinea-fowl chicks** (helmeted guineafowl): grey with white dots; one has a crooked crest.
- **Warthog twins:** small tusks, tufty manes; one always has a muddy nose.

## The real animals
- **Giraffe** (*Giraffa*): the tallest land animal; lives in African savannas; browses acacia leaves with a long prehensile tongue; must splay its front legs to drink. The horn-like knobs are called ossicones. (Check current species taxonomy before giving a number of species.)
- **Meerkat** (*Suricata suricatta*): a small mongoose of southern African dry lands (Kalahari, Namib edges); lives in groups ("mobs"); takes turns as sentinel, standing upright.
- **Leopard tortoise** (*Stigmochelys pardalis*): large tortoise of eastern and southern African savanna.
- **Helmeted guineafowl** (*Numida meleagris*): ground bird of African savanna.
- **Common warthog** (*Phacochoerus africanus*): kneels to feed; wallows in mud.
- Trees: acacias (many now called *Vachellia*), baobab.

## Setting and environment
A Southern African savanna (bushveld) in the late dry season: heat, dust, a waterhole with a steep muddy bank, acacia trees, an old baobab, tall grass, a hill for sunsets. Ends with the first rain and fresh green growth.

## The value
For a child: to understand a friend, go to where they are and see what they see. For grown-ups: empathy as perspective-taking, and it runs both ways (Langa learns Themba's view and shares his own). He listens before he fixes, and finally notices what isn't said out loud. Avoids: the big one rescuing the little ones, and casting Langa as a bully.

## Where the value comes from
**Ubuntu** (Southern Africa, Nguni peoples: Zulu, Xhosa, Ndebele, Swazi; Sotho-Tswana *botho*). Saying: *Umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu*, "a person is a person through other people". The everyday isiZulu greeting *Sawubona*, "we see you", answered *Ngikhona*, "I am here". Sources and reliability: `values-library.json` key "14" (row southern_africa-01 and related rows). Names: Langa means "sun", Themba means "hope" (isiZulu/isiXhosa). The story text itself uses no Zulu words.

## Key moments
1. Langa, tallest of all, races to the waterhole each morning.
2. His dust makes Themba sneeze; Langa, head in the breeze, thinks everything is fine.
3. He eats the high pods; suggests the small ones "just stretch".
4. Follow-the-Leader leaves everyone behind; Themba says he can't see it from up there.
5. Turning point: the giraffe bow under the moon by the baobab: "Show me."
6. Down in the grass forest, dust in his own eyes.
7. He listens to each small animal's problem.
8. He makes mud steps, bends branches, walks softly.
9. He notices Themba silent on tiptoe at sunset and invites him up.
10. Climax: the shared view between his horns.
11. First rain; gentle steps.

## Refrain and theme line
- Refrain: "Clomp, clomp, clomp!" (loud at first, soft at the end)
- Theme line: "When we truly see each other, we all grow taller."

## Questions to ask
1. Why didn't Langa notice that his friends were unhappy?
2. How did it feel for Langa to bend all the way down?
3. What did he learn when he was down low?
4. How did he know Themba wanted to see the sunset, even though Themba said nothing?
5. When could you "bend down" to see how a friend feels?
