## Story in short
Venny the beaver and Sammy the squirrel live beside a wide, fast river and spot a huge strawberry bush on the far bank. They agree to build a bridge but not on how: Venny wants heavy, solid logs, Sammy wants light branches so it's quick. After a long argument each builds his own. Venny's logs drift away and Sammy's branches wobble and snap. Wet and grumpy, they blame each other, until Sammy wonders if they should try together. Each sees what's good in the other's plan: logs are strong but too heavy alone, branches are bendy but too weak alone. Sammy suggests tying the logs together with the branches. Laughing as they work, they build the sturdiest bridge the forest has seen, cross it and share the strawberries, and stay best friends who know teamwork makes things better and more fun.

## Characters
- **Venny** (beaver): strong, practical, likes things solid and sturdy; taps his thick tail when he talks. Wants a bridge that lasts.
- **Sammy** (squirrel): quick, light on his feet, likes fast and easy solutions. Has the idea that joins both plans.
- Both are main characters; neither is "right". Look details are not in the text (check the illustrations).

## The real animals
- Beaver (species not named; North American beaver, *Castor canadensis*, or Eurasian beaver, *Castor fiber* (check)): beavers live in rivers, streams and ponds, cut trees with their teeth, and build dams and lodges from logs, branches, mud and stones. North American beavers are found across most of Canada and the US; Eurasian beavers in Europe and parts of Asia. Beavers really use their flat tails (for balance, swimming and slapping the water as a warning).
- Squirrel (species not named; e.g. eastern grey squirrel, *Sciurus carolinensis*, or red squirrel (check)): tree squirrels build nests called dreys from twigs and leaves, so Sammy's light-branch idea fits the real animal nicely.
- Fun contrast for nature articles: a beaver's building material (heavy logs packed with mud) and a squirrel's (light twigs woven together) mirror their two ideas in the book.

## Setting and environment
A peaceful forest on the bank of a wide, rushing river, on a single day (morning argument, building, success by evening). The goal is a large strawberry bush on the opposite bank. Landmarks: the riverbank, the two failed bridges, the final log-and-branch bridge.

## The value
For a child: when we put our ideas together, we can do more than either of us could alone.
For a grown-up: teamwork here grows out of a real disagreement. Each friend has a good idea with a weak spot; the turning point comes when they stop blaming and start naming what is good in the other's plan. It models listening, giving credit and combining ideas.
What the book avoids: one friend being right and the other wrong; teamwork as simply doing what the other person says.

## Where the value comes from
Shared across traditions. Related sayings:
- **Ancient Greece & Rome**: a father shows his quarrelling sons that single sticks snap easily but a tied bundle can't be broken; "Union gives strength." Source: Aesop, The Old Man and his Sons / The Bundle of Sticks (Perry Index 53; Babrius), moral as worded by J. Jacobs (1894). care_flag: none.
- **Korea**: "Even a sheet of paper is lighter when two people lift it together." Original: 백지장도 맞들면 낫다. Source: traditional Korean proverb (속담); Indiana University Korean-program proverb list (item 16). care_flag: none.
- **Celtic Ireland**: "There is no strength without unity" (literally, "no strength without putting together"). Original: Ní neart go cur le chéile. Source: Irish proverb, Ó Dónaill, Foclóir Gaeilge–Béarla (1977), on teanglann.ie, s.v. neart. care_flag: none.
- Also in the library and very close to this book: the Q'eswachaka grass-rope bridge, rebuilt each year by four Quechua communities who twist thin cords into great ropes (UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, 2013). care_flag: cultural reader advised; it includes rituals that should not be depicted. ## Key moments
1. Venny and Sammy live by a wide river and see a giant strawberry bush on the other side.
2. They agree to build a bridge but argue about how: heavy logs or light branches.
3. They split up and each builds his own bridge.
4. Venny's logs float away; Sammy's branches collapse.
5. Wet and cross, they blame each other, then Sammy suggests trying together.
6. Each names what is strong and weak in the other's idea; Sammy thinks of tying logs with branches.
7. They build together, laughing, and finish a sturdy, beautiful bridge.
8. They cross, share the strawberries and stay best friends.

## Refrain and theme line
- Refrain: none.
- Theme line: "Together, we can do more."

## Questions to ask
1. Why couldn't Venny and Sammy agree at first?
2. What went wrong with each bridge when they built alone?
3. What was good about Venny's idea? What was good about Sammy's?
4. When have you and a friend had different ideas? What did you do?
5. What could you build with someone else this week?

## Notes
- Wild strawberries grow as low plants, not tall bushes; the "strawberry bush" is a playful bend (there is a shrub actually called strawberry bush, *Euonymus americanus*, but it is not edible (check) — don't confuse the two in nature articles).
- Children should never build or cross makeshift bridges over real rivers; keep activities to table-top or sandbox bridges with a grown-up.
- The book's subtitle is simply "A story about Teamwork".
