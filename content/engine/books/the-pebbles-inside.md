## Story in short
Tavi is a little turtle with a big, quiet heart who lives by the willow brook. Her best friend Fern, a long-eared hare, says whatever she feels straight away. When frogs tease Tavi for being slow, she says she's fine and tucks the hurt inside like a pebble. She does the same with everything that feels risky to say: a worry about thunder, a "no, thank you", a great game idea, even telling Fern she's her best friend. Pebble by pebble her shell grows so heavy she can't climb Clover Hill or play, and she can't find her giggle. One evening Fern sits beside her and gently asks; when Tavi says she's fine, Fern simply stays. Under the stars Tavi shares one hurt, then another, and Fern listens kindly. Lighter, they set the colourful pebbles in the brook, the frog says sorry, others add their own, and together they make a path of stepping stones.

## Characters
- **Tavi** (turtle, she): smooth green shell, big quiet heart; says "I'm fine" and keeps her feelings inside; learns to share them with a safe friend. The hero.
- **Fern** (hare, she): long ears, says what she feels right away; a patient, gentle listener who doesn't push. The friend who listens.
- **The little frog**: one of the frogs who teased Tavi; later admits he laughed because he was scared of being laughed at, and says sorry.
- **The duck and the hedgehog**: bring their own pebbles; part of the community that joins in.

## The real animals
- Turtle (species not named; a small freshwater turtle living by a brook, e.g. a painted turtle, *Chrysemys picta*, in North America, or a European pond turtle, *Emys orbicularis* (check illustrations)): freshwater turtles live in ponds, slow streams and marshes, bask on logs and stones, and pull their head and legs into their shell for protection. They are slow on land but good swimmers. Many brumate (a kind of winter rest) at the bottom of ponds in cold climates (check).
- Hare (species not named; European hare, *Lepus europaeus*, or a North American hare (check)): hares are larger than rabbits with longer ears and legs, live above ground in shallow hollows called forms, and are very fast runners. The turtle-and-hare friendship playfully nods to Aesop's The Tortoise and the Hare, but here they are friends, not rivals.
- Frogs, duck, hedgehog: brook and meadow neighbours. A hedgehog suggests a European setting (hedgehogs are not native to the Americas) (check against illustrations).

## Setting and environment
The willow brook: a gentle stream with willows on its banks, grass beside it, Clover Hill nearby, frogs at the water's edge, and stones in the stream (hop-stones). Time passes over days; the key scene is one evening as the stars come out. A thunderstorm is mentioned as one of Tavi's worries.

## The value
For a child: feelings we keep inside can feel heavy; telling someone we trust makes them lighter, and good friends listen.
For a grown-up: the book shows why children hide feelings (fear of being laughed at, being wrong, hearing no) and what it costs. It models what a safe listener does: notices, asks gently, doesn't push, stays, thanks the child for telling, and doesn't laugh. Shared feelings also become something good together (the path), and the teaser's own feelings are part of it.
What the book avoids: forcing a child to talk; treating quiet children as a problem; saying feelings are bad. Happy feelings and ideas count too, not just hurts.

## Key moments
1. Tavi the quiet turtle and Fern the outspoken hare are best friends by the willow brook.
2. Frogs tease Tavi for being slow; she says she's fine and tucks the hurt inside like a pebble.
3. She tucks away more: wanting to tell Fern she is her best friend, a thunder worry, a "no", a game idea.
4. Her shell grows heavy; she can't climb Clover Hill or play hop-stones, and can't find her giggle.
5. Fern sits with her, asks gently, and when Tavi says she's fine, simply stays.
6. Under the stars Tavi shares the first hurt; Fern thanks her and understands, and the weight lifts.
7. More feelings come out, ending with "you're my best friend", and Fern says it back.
8. They lay the pebbles in the brook; the frog apologises, others add pebbles, and the stepping stones become a path for everyone.

## Refrain and theme line
- Refrain: "I'm fine." (Tavi's repeated answer) and the sound "Click." each time she tucks a feeling away.
- Theme line: "Feelings kept inside grow heavy. Feelings we share become a path."

## Questions to ask
1. Why did Tavi say "I'm fine" when she wasn't?
2. What happened to Tavi's shell as she kept more feelings inside?
3. What did Fern do that helped Tavi feel safe to talk?
4. Why do you think the frog laughed at Tavi? What did he do later?
5. Who could you tell when you have a "pebble" inside? What kind of pebble would you like to share today?

## Notes
- **Safety:** no therapy or diagnosis advice. For a child who seems withdrawn or anxious for a long time, articles should suggest talking to the child's doctor or educator.
