## Story in short
Lumi is the smallest firefly in the meadow, with the warmest glow; her best friend Moss is a moth who can't glow but loves her light. Wise Old Glow warns that the Night of No Moon is coming, when every light will be needed. As some fireflies' lights fade, Lumi fears hers will run out, so she hides in a hollow log with her glow under a leaf. The meadow grows dark: the mice trip and the snail loses its way. On the darkest night Moss searches for her and gets tangled in brambles. Trembling, Lumi uncovers her light and guides him free. Then she shares her glow with a firefly whose light has gone out, and his light blooms while hers grows brighter. Firefly by firefly, a river of light fills the meadow, everyone finds their way, and the whole meadow starts sharing what it has.

## Characters
- **Lumi** (firefly, she): the smallest, with the warmest glow; afraid of running out, so she hides her light; finds the courage to shine and share. The hero.
- **Moss** (moth, he): Lumi's best friend; can't glow but loves her light; brave enough to go looking for her and gets lost.
- **Old Glow** (glowworm, she): wise elder of the meadow; announces the Night of No Moon and names the lesson.
- **The little firefly** (he): his light has gone out; Lumi relights it.
- **Meadow neighbours**: the mouse family, the snail, a lost duckling and her mother, the hedgehog, the cricket.

## The real animals
- Firefly (beetles of the family Lampyridae; species not named (check)): fireflies are beetles, not flies. They make light by a chemical reaction in their bodies (bioluminescence, using luciferin and the enzyme luciferase), and flash patterns help them find mates. They live in warm, damp places such as meadows, marshes and woodland edges on most continents. Firefly numbers are falling in many places because of habitat loss, pesticides and light pollution (check before stating).
- Glowworm (in Europe, the common glow-worm, *Lampyris noctiluca*, is itself a firefly-family beetle whose wingless females glow (check); in Australia and New Zealand "glowworms" are fungus gnat larvae). Old Glow is probably meant as the European kind.
- Moth (species not named): moths are mostly night-flying insects; they don't glow. Many are drawn to artificial lights, which can harm them (check).
- Hedgehog (likely the European hedgehog, *Erinaceus europaeus* (check)): hedgehogs are not native to the Americas, so the meadow reads as European. Hedgehogs are nocturnal and hibernate in winter.
- Snail, field mice, cricket (crickets "sing" by rubbing their wings together), duck and duckling: common meadow and pond-edge neighbours.

## Setting and environment
A meadow on summer nights, cooling toward late summer, with a hollow log, brambles, stones and paths, a pond edge (duckling), and a dandelion where Lumi and Moss watch the sunrise. The key event is the Night of No Moon (a new moon, when the night is darkest).

## The value
For a child: kindness is a light you can share, and sharing it doesn't use it up; it makes more.
For a grown-up: the book names a real fear (if I give, I'll have less) and shows the opposite: helping others makes Lumi's light bigger. It links kindness and courage (she has to come out of hiding to help). Kindness spreads: once Lumi shares, the whole meadow begins giving in its own way.
What the book avoids: kindness as self-sacrifice until empty; shaming Lumi for being afraid.

## Where the value comes from
Shared across traditions. Related sayings:
- **Japan**: kindness to others is not only for their sake; it goes around and comes back to you. Original: 情けは人の為ならず (nasake wa hito no tame narazu). Source: Japanese proverb (kotowaza); Digital Daijisen (Shogakukan) and Kotowaza o shiru jiten, via Kotobank. Often misread as "kindness does others no good"; use the dictionary meaning. care_flag: none.
- **Māori (Aotearoa New Zealand)**: "If kindness is sown, then kindness you shall receive." Original: He aroha whakatō, he aroha puta mai. Source: Massey University, 'Whakataukī – Māori proverbs' list; same gloss on the Playcentre Aotearoa whakataukī sheet. care_flag: cultural reader advised (if an article uses this one, write it with `draft: true`).

## Refrain and theme line
- Refrain: none (the sound words "Blink! Blink! Blink!" open the book).
- Theme line: "Some gifts grow bigger every time you give them away."

## Questions to ask
1. Why did Lumi hide her light?
2. How did the meadow change when the fireflies stopped shining?
3. What made Lumi brave enough to come out of the log?
4. What happened to Lumi's light when she shared it? Were you surprised?
5. What is a "light" you have that you could share with someone (a smile, a game, help)?
