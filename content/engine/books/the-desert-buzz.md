## Story in short
In a hot, sandy desert, a tiny beetle called Etoo and a bee called Bella are best friends who climb cactus plants, share nectar and rest under cool rocks. One scorching afternoon Etoo builds a sand tower while Bella gathers nectar. When she zooms past, the breeze from her wings knocks it flat. Etoo explodes, shouting that she ruins everything. Bella says it was an accident, but he won't listen and stomps off, and she flies away hurt. Alone under a rock, Etoo notices how empty it feels without her and that staying angry isn't helping. He takes a slow, deep breath and remembers a wise old scorpion's advice about pausing and listening to your heart. He finds Bella and apologises; she says sorry too. They decide to build the biggest tower yet together, and Etoo learns that a deep breath helps him cool down.

## Characters
- **Etoo** (beetle): tiny, proud of his sand tower, quick-tempered; the one who learns to calm down. The hero.
- **Bella** (bee): cheerful and busy; didn't mean any harm, is hurt by Etoo's words, and quickly makes up.
- **The wise old scorpion** (mentioned, not seen in the action): the source of the advice Etoo remembers.

## The real animals
- Beetle (species not named (check)): deserts are home to many beetles, especially darkling beetles (family Tenebrionidae). A famous example is the Namib Desert fog-basking beetle (*Onymacris unguicularis*), which collects drinking water from fog on its back (check). Many desert beetles shelter under rocks or in sand during the hottest hours, just like Etoo and Bella.
- Bee (species not named; honey bee, *Apis mellifera*, or a native desert bee (check)): deserts such as the Sonoran have hundreds of native bee species that visit cactus flowers (check numbers before using). Bees fly by beating their wings very fast, which is what makes the buzz.
- Scorpion (species not named (check)): scorpions live in deserts on every continent except Antarctica, are mostly active at night and glow under ultraviolet light (check). Some species can sting dangerously; articles should say to look, not touch.
- Cactus: cacti are native to the Americas (with one possible exception (check)), so a desert with cacti points to places like the Sonoran, Mojave or Chihuahuan deserts.

## Setting and environment
A hot, sandy desert of dunes, cactus plants, flowers with nectar and cool rocks for shade, on one scorching afternoon. The desert breeze after a sandstorm and the cool wind after a hot day are used as images for calming down.

## The value
For a child: it's okay to feel angry, but when anger gets big, stop, take a deep breath and listen to your heart before you act.
For a grown-up: the book separates the feeling (anger is natural; Etoo's tower really was knocked down) from what we do with it (shouting "you always ruin everything", stomping off). It shows the cost of an outburst (loneliness), a simple calming tool (a slow breath), and repair (both friends say sorry).
What the book avoids: "don't be angry" or shame for feeling angry; the idea that the accident was Bella's fault.

## Where the value comes from
Shared across traditions. Related sayings:
- **Ancient Egypt**: the heated man is "like a tree growing indoors", while the truly calm man is "like a tree grown in a meadow" that grows green and gives fruit and shade. Source: Instruction of Amenemope, ch. 4 (Lichtheim, AEL II, 1976; COS 1.47); P. BM EA 10474. care_flag: none.
- **West Africa (Igbo)**: "A soft word causes anger to turn back." Source: F. C. Ogbalu, Ilu Igbo: The Book of Igbo Proverbs (Onitsha: Varsity Press, 1965), 'Kindness and Wickedness' no. 7; English draft translation by Frances W. Pritchett. care_flag: none.

## Questions to ask
1. Why did Etoo get so angry?
2. Was it fair when Etoo said Bella always ruins everything? Why or why not?
3. How do you think Bella felt when Etoo stomped away?
4. What helped Etoo calm down?
5. What helps you when you feel a big angry feeling? Shall we practise a slow breath together?

## Notes
- The title page calls it "a story about managing anger"; books.js value key is `anger` and the moment is `big-feelings`. Frame it as managing anger, never "being angry is bad".
- Safety: no medical or therapy advice. For frequent or intense outbursts, articles should suggest talking to the child's doctor or educator.
- A bee's wing breeze toppling a sand tower is a playful exaggeration.
- Real scorpions can be dangerous; if mentioned, say children should never touch or pick up scorpions, beetles in the wild, or bees.
