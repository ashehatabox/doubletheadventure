## Story in short
Tama is the littlest of the little blue penguins on a New Zealand beach. Every evening the colony swims home and walks up a sandy path to their burrows. One night a storm covers the path with seaweed, driftwood, sticks and a lost flip-flop, and the penguins stumble home grumbling that someone should clean it up. Nobody does, and Tama hops over it saying it isn't his mess. Then he helps a tiny chick stuck behind driftwood, and that night he can't sleep: his great-great-grandmother walked this path, and one day that chick's chicks will too. He decides it doesn't matter whose mess it is; he will look after the path. His kiwi friend Aroha laughs that he's the littlest penguin in the world, so he starts little: one stick, then two. Aroha pushes the big driftwood with her strong legs. Every day Tama clears what the sea brings and lines the edges with white shells that glow in the moonlight. Three chicks ask to help, and he teaches them to start little. Under a silver moon the colony walks home and nobody trips.

## Characters
- **Tama** (little blue penguin / kororā): slate-blue back, white tummy, pale eyes, pink-grey feet, a tuft of baby down on his head.
- **Aroha** (brown kiwi): shaggy feathers, long thin beak, strong legs, always carries a shiny blue-green shell (paua-like) she puts down to help.
- **Three chicks:** fluffy grey-brown penguin chicks; one has a white feather on her chest (the one Tama helped).
- **The colony:** adult little blue penguins walking home in a line.

## The real animals
- **Little penguin / kororā** (*Eudyptula minor*): the smallest penguin species (about 30 cm tall); lives around New Zealand and southern Australia; feeds at sea by day and comes ashore after dusk, walking in groups to burrows in dunes and coastal scrub. Threats include dogs, cats, stoats and traffic near nesting areas. (Check details.)
- **Kiwi** (*Apteryx*): flightless, nocturnal birds found only in New Zealand; nostrils at the tip of the long beak; strong legs; most species threatened. Some forage near coasts (check which).
- Coastal trees with red flowers in early summer: pōhutukawa.

## Setting and environment
A small New Zealand beach and dunes, from an autumn storm through winter to spring chicks and early summer (red pōhutukawa flowers). The shell-lined sandy path from the sea to burrows under the bushes is the central place.

## The value
For a child: look after the places that look after you, a little every day, even if it's not your mess. For grown-ups: guardianship as daily, unglamorous care for a shared place, started small and passed on to the next generation. Avoids: blaming people or lecturing about pollution (the mess comes from a storm; the only human trace is one flip-flop), doing it for praise ("nobody noticed"), the lonely martyr, and unsafe litter-picking (only soft, harmless things; nothing sharp).

## Where the value comes from
**Kaitiakitanga** (Māori, Aotearoa New Zealand): guardianship of the land, sea and living things, caring for them as ancestors did and handing them on cared for.
