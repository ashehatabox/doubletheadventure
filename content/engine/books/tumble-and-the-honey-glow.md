## Story in short
Tumble is a bouncy bear cub who rolls and tumbles everywhere, and the whole forest is getting ready for the Last Leaf Games. One golden autumn day a tickly cough starts in his throat, and Tumble decides a "Tickle Monster" has got inside him that he must fight. So he roars at his shadow, races the wind and climbs the tallest rock, but by afternoon he is tired and sniffly. Zuzu the honeybee wonders whether all that racing is helping at all. Mama Bear doesn't tell him what to do; she asks him what his body needs right now, a question he has never asked himself. In the den he listens and chooses warm soup, berries and a soft blanket, and Zuzu brings honey from the hive for Mama to stir into warm milk. When the games begin outside, Tumble stays in to rest, and coughs into his elbow to keep his friends well. By morning the cough is soft. When the first snow falls he joins the very last game, and shares his honey cup with a friend who is coughing.

## Characters
- **Tumble** (bear cub): bouncy, eager, a red knitted scarf; thinks stopping means being weak and missing out. The hero.
- **Zuzu** (honeybee): tiny, cheerful, one stripy leg warmer; gently questions Tumble's fighting and brings honey from the hive.
- **Mama Bear**: calm, a green shawl; never rescues or orders, only asks the question that turns the story.
- **Forest friends**: get ready for and play in the Last Leaf Games.

## The real animals
- Bear (species not named; the art suggests a brown bear, *Ursus arctos*, or an American black bear, *Ursus americanus* (check)): bears eat a lot in autumn to build fat for winter, and many sleep through much of the cold months in a den. Writers can link this to resting as something strong animals do on purpose (check facts before using).
- Honeybee (*Apis mellifera*): lives in colonies; worker bees make honey from flower nectar and store it to feed the hive through winter. A honeybee visits many flowers to make a tiny amount of honey (check figures before using).

## Setting and environment
A northern forest across three seasons: golden autumn with falling leaves and hills to roll down, cold rain, then the first snow. Landmarks: the bears' cozy den, the tall rock, the bees' hive, the field where the Last Leaf Games are played. The story moves from bright outdoor play to the warm, quiet den, and back out into the snow.

## The value
For a child: listening to your body means stopping to ask "What does my body need right now?", and resting when you're unwell is a strong thing to do, not a weak one.
For a grown-up: the book separates strength from pushing through. Fighting the cough makes Tumble worse; noticing, resting and accepting care help him. His body does the healing; he helps it. He still cares for others (coughing into his elbow, sharing his cup) and doesn't lose everything by resting: he joins the last game.
What the book avoids: "sick means weak" or "fight harder"; honey as medicine (it soothes, rest and time heal); a scary monster (it is only Tumble's idea); a grown-up rescue (Mama only asks, Tumble chooses).

## Where the value comes from
Shared across traditions; keep it secular. The idea that caring for your body is part of being strong, and the long home tradition of soothing a cough with warmth, rest and honey, appear in many cultures (for example in old Egyptian, Arab and Indian home care (check before naming any one)). Don't present honey as a cure and don't name religious sources.

## Refrain and theme line
- Refrain: "Warm, slow, honey-glow."
- Theme line: "The strongest bears are gentle with themselves."

## Questions to ask
1. Why did Tumble think he had to fight the Tickle Monster?
2. What happened when he raced and climbed? How did his body feel?
3. What question did Mama Bear ask? What would your body say if you asked it right now?
4. Why did Tumble cough into his elbow?
5. Was resting brave? Why do you think so?

## Notes
- Safety, always: never give honey to babies under 1 year old; see a doctor if a cough lasts or breathing is hard. Put this in every article that mentions honey. No medical advice beyond that; for real worries, suggest the child's doctor.
- Activities: warm drinks only with a grown-up and not too hot; no honey activities for children under 1 or with allergies noted.
- The Tickle Monster is never scary: describe it as Tumble's idea, not a real creature.
- The dedication honours "every little one brave enough to rest". Authors: Amr Mohamed and Abdallah Shehata.
