## Story in short
Quilla, a young porcupine, moves with her family into an empty burrow in Bramble Hollow just as the first cold wind arrives. The village animals keep warm by squeezing together under the Big Oak, but when Quilla asks to join, they shuffle away because her quills look prickly, and she ends up alone at the meadow's edge. Bodie the badger, whose favourite thing is a cosy squeeze, notices her. He is nervous that the others might shun him too, but he crosses the meadow and sits beside her anyway. After a long quiet look at the oak, Quilla decides she has waited long enough to be invited: they will build a warm place of their own, right there. Bodie digs, Quilla climbs for vines, and her own loose quills become the pins that hold the walls. A squirrel, a mouse and a mole join because the project looks like fun. When the hut is finished, someone asks who it is for, and Quilla, remembering how it felt to be left out, says it is for everyone. On the first snowy night the whole village comes, including the rabbit twins who had turned her away.

## Characters
- **Quilla** (porcupine): new in the village, shy; her quills stand up when she feels shy and lie flat when she is happy. Good climber. Wears a yellow neckerchief.
- **Bodie** (European badger): young badger with a black-and-white striped face, grey coat and blue knitted bobble hat. Loves being snug; a digger. The first to cross the meadow.
- **Hazel** (red squirrel): bushy tail, acorn-cup cap; brings moss and leaves.
- **Thimble** (field/wood mouse): wears a thimble as a hat; ties tiny knots.
- **Mossy** (mole): velvet coat, tiny round glasses; smooths the floor.
- **Fern and Fig** (rabbits): twins who first call Quilla "too prickly" and later say sorry; Fern has a daisy behind one ear, Fig a red knitted scarf.

## The real animals
- **Porcupines:** two unrelated families: Old World porcupines (Africa, southern Europe, Asia) live on the ground; New World porcupines (the Americas, including the North American porcupine in Canada) climb trees well. Quills are modified hairs; porcupines cannot shoot them, but loose quills detach easily. Herbivores (bark, leaves, roots, fruit). (Check which species best matches the book's art before naming one.)
- **European badger** (*Meles meles*): lives in family groups in underground setts with many tunnels and chambers across Europe; strong digger; mostly nocturnal; eats earthworms and many other foods.
- **Red squirrel, field mouse, mole, rabbits:** woodland and meadow animals of Europe; moles live almost entirely underground and dig with broad front paws.

## Setting and environment
Bramble Hollow: a meadow and woodland village with a Big Oak, a stone wall and brambles, in late autumn moving into the first snow. Seasonal arc: first cold wind, frost, wind, first snowfall. The hut ("the Snug") is a round vine hut at the meadow's edge, roofed with moss, pinned with quills, with acorn-cup lanterns by a low round door.

## The value
For a child: if there's no room for you, you can make a place, and then make room for everyone. For grown-ups: belonging you build yourself, without waiting for permission, and the real test is whether you open your own door, even to those who left you out. The book deliberately avoids "you belong once you're useful": nobody lets Quilla in because her quills turn out to be handy, nobody is rescued by the hut, and the door is declared open to everyone before anyone apologises.

## Questions to ask
1. How do you think Quilla felt sitting at the edge of the meadow?
2. Why was it brave for Bodie to walk over and sit with her?
3. What did Quilla decide to do instead of waiting?
4. Why do you think she said the Snug was for everyone?
5. Is there someone who might like you to sit with them?

## Notes
- No elder or grown-up solves anything; Bodie's courage and Quilla's choice carry the story.
- The rabbits' apology is repair, not a ticket in; avoid framing it as "they were let in because they said sorry".
- Good tie-ins: starting a new school, moving house, newcomers (the site has an educators page for newcomers).
