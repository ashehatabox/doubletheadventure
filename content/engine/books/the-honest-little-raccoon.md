## Story in short
Riley, a clever, curious young raccoon, explores a busy city's alleys and rooftops at night. He meets Maggie, an old pigeon who seems calm about everything; she tells him trouble passes but the truth stays with you, and that honesty, though hard, opens doors lies can't. Riley thinks about this while roaming with his best friend Benny, a big, strong raccoon who always protects him. One chilly night the smell of fresh bread draws them through a bakery window, and they nibble the sweet rolls until Riley's tail knocks a tray over with a crash. The baker comes running. Benny whispers that he'll take the blame so Riley can get away. Remembering Maggie's words, Riley steps forward, admits it was him and says sorry. The baker praises his courage and tells them to ask next time. Riley leaves feeling light and free, and becomes known as the honest little raccoon.

## Characters
- **Riley** (raccoon, he): clever, curious, nimble; makes a mistake, then chooses to tell the truth even when he could escape. The hero.
- **Benny** (raccoon): Riley's big, strong best friend; protective, offers to take the blame. Surprised by Riley's honesty.
- **Maggie** (pigeon): wise old city bird and Riley's mentor. Called "she", though one line says "his feathers" (text inconsistency; use she).
- **The baker** (human): startled and cross at first, then moved by Riley's honesty; kind in the end.

## The real animals
- Raccoon (*Procyon lotor*): native to North America, from southern Canada through the US to Central America; introduced in parts of Europe and Japan. Raccoons are mostly nocturnal, very good at using their front paws, and have adapted well to cities, where they live in attics, sewers and parks and search bins for food. Their black "mask" and ringed tail are their best-known features.
- Pigeon (rock dove / feral pigeon, *Columba livia*): one of the most common city birds worldwide; city pigeons descend from domesticated rock doves. Pigeons are known for their homing ability. Note: pigeons are active by day and roost at night, so an evening chat is a mild story bend.

## Setting and environment
A bustling city at night, under streetlights: alleyways, rooftops, chilly evening streets, and a small bakery with an open window, a counter of sweet rolls and trays. Season looks like late autumn or winter (chilly evenings; check illustrations).

## The value
For a child: telling the truth can feel scary, but it makes your heart feel light and helps people trust you.
For a grown-up: Riley's honesty comes at a real moment of choice: someone he loves offers him a way out. The book shows that owning up includes saying sorry and explaining, and that a grown-up can respond to honesty with kindness. It also gently shows that one lie leads to more.
What the book avoids: honesty as a way to avoid punishment; a harsh or shaming grown-up. Writers should also not suggest that the raccoons were right to sneak in; the story is about owning a mistake.

## Where the value comes from
Shared across traditions. Related sayings:
- **Ancient Greece & Rome**: a shepherd boy cries "Wolf!" as a joke; when a real wolf comes, nobody believes him. "A liar will not be believed, even when he speaks the truth." Source: Aesop, The Shepherd Who Cried 'Wolf!' in Jest (Perry Index 210), moral as worded by J. Jacobs (1894). care_flag: none.
- **Ancient India**: "Truth alone triumphs, not falsehood." Original: satyam eva jayate nānṛtam. Source: Mundaka Upanishad 3.1.6 (also the national motto of India). care_flag: none.
- The values library also tags a Freemasonry row directly to this book: "Truth is a Divine attribute, and the foundation of every virtue" (Thomas Smith Webb, The Freemason's Monitor, 1797; 1865 ed., p. 41). care_flag: none. Less child-friendly; use only if it fits.

## Key moments
1. Riley, a curious city raccoon, explores at night.
2. He meets Maggie the old pigeon, who tells him the truth stays with you and opens doors.
3. Riley wanders with his big, protective best friend Benny.
4. The smell of bread draws them through a bakery window; they nibble sweet rolls.
5. Riley's tail knocks over a tray; the baker rushes in.
6. Benny offers to take the blame so Riley can go.
7. Riley remembers Maggie's advice, owns up and apologises.
8. The baker praises his honesty and tells them to ask next time; Riley feels free and explains to Benny why he told the truth.

## Refrain and theme line
- Refrain: "Truth will always set you free." (Maggie says it; Riley remembers it at the key moment and echoes it at the end)
- Theme line: "Lying only traps you in more lies. But the truth? It set me free."

## Questions to ask
1. What did Maggie tell Riley about the truth?
2. Why do you think Benny offered to take the blame?
3. How did Riley feel just before he told the truth? How did he feel afterwards?
4. What did the baker do when Riley was honest?
5. Can you remember a time when telling the truth was hard? What happened?

## Notes
- Secular rule: don't connect the refrain to any religious text.
- The baker promises to leave treats outside. Real-life wildlife guidance: people should not feed raccoons or other wild animals (check local guidance). Don't recommend it.
- Raccoons can carry diseases; children should never approach or touch them.
- The sneaking-in is the mistake, not a fun idea; don't frame it as an adventure to copy.
