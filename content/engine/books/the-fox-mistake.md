## Story in short
Felix and Finn are best-friend foxes who race through the autumn woods, share fruit and live in dens side by side. Felix finds a beautiful shiny pebble by the river and keeps it in his den, but the next day it's gone. Seeing a shiny pebble outside Finn's den, he accuses Finn of taking it. Finn says he found his own near the oak tree, but Felix calls him a liar. Hurt and angry, they turn away from each other, and for days both are miserable. Then one evening Felix spots his own pebble glinting in the river: it must have rolled out of his den. Ashamed, he goes straight to Finn, explains and says sorry for not believing him. Finn tells him how much it hurt, and forgives him. They hug and stay friends, knowing mistakes happen but forgiveness can make a friendship stronger.

## Characters
- **Felix** (fox): proud of his special find, quick to blame; makes the mistake, then owns it and apologises. The one who learns.
- **Finn** (fox): honest and loyal; deeply hurt by being called a liar, but chooses to forgive. The one who forgives.

## The real animals
- Fox (species not named; most likely the red fox, *Vulpes vulpes* (check)): the red fox lives across North America, Europe, Asia and North Africa, in forests, fields and even cities. It is the most widespread wild member of the dog family. Foxes dig or borrow dens, mainly to raise their kits. Real foxes are mostly solitary outside the breeding season, so two fox friends living side by side is a story bend.
- Foxes do hide food in small caches to eat later (true; check details before quoting), which could link playfully to Felix keeping his treasure in his den. They don't collect pebbles.

## Setting and environment
A quiet forest in autumn, under golden falling leaves, with a river running through it and an oak tree nearby. Two dens side by side. The story covers several days, ending on an evening walk by the river.

## The value
For a child: everyone makes mistakes; when we're wrong we say sorry, and when someone says sorry we can forgive them.
For a grown-up: the book shows both halves of repair. Felix makes a false accusation, notices his error, feels ashamed and apologises clearly (he names what he did). Finn tells the truth about his hurt before forgiving. Forgiveness doesn't mean pretending it didn't hurt.
What the book avoids: forgiveness as "it's fine, forget it"; the hurt person being rushed. It also avoids a villain: nobody stole anything.

## Where the value comes from
Shared across traditions. Related sayings:
- **Ancient China**: the jealous general Lian Po learned that Lin Xiangru had avoided quarrels to protect their kingdom; ashamed, he carried thorny brambles on his bare back to Lin's door and asked forgiveness, and they became firm friends. ## Questions to ask
1. Why did Felix think Finn took his pebble?
2. How do you think Finn felt when Felix didn't believe him?
3. What did Felix do when he found out he was wrong?
4. Why do you think Finn forgave him?
5. What can you say or do when you have made a mistake with a friend?

## Notes
- This book's value is forgiveness, but it is equally about apologising and about not jumping to conclusions. Moment articles can cover "saying sorry" (books.js moments: sorry, friendship).
- Writers: Finn first tells Felix he was hurt, then forgives. Keep that order; don't teach children they must forgive instantly.
- River safety: Felix spots the pebble in the water. In activities, no wading or reaching into rivers without a grown-up.
- Real foxes are wild animals; don't suggest approaching or feeding them.
