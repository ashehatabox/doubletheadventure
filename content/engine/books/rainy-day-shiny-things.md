## Story in short
Three cats share a cosy house on Maple Street with kind Mrs. Maple, who tucks each one in at night: curious Whiskers, who explores every cupboard and shelf; quiet Shadow, who loves napping in sunbeams; and playful Mittens, who chases yarn and pounces on shadows. On a rainy day all three are grumpy. Whiskers wants to chase a butterfly outside, Shadow misses the sun and can't settle, and Mittens finds her toy mouse dull. Then a crash from the kitchen: a glass has fallen and broken, and among the sparkling pieces they spot the silver wings of Mrs. Maple's special necklace, a gift from her husband. Their bad mood vanishes. At dinner they purr and rub against Mrs. Maple, noticing how much love and care they have. Curled up together on the windowsill, they now find the rain soothing, and Whiskers decides every day holds something special.

## Characters
- **Whiskers** (domestic cat, he): curious, always exploring; frustrated by the rain because he wants to chase a butterfly. Speaks the book's lesson.
- **Mittens** (domestic cat, she): playful and energetic; bored with her toy mouse indoors.
- **Shadow** (domestic cat, he): quiet observer who loves warm sunny naps; restless without the sun.
- **Mrs. Maple** (human): kind, caring owner who tucks the cats in each night and feeds them; her necklace with silver wings was a gift from her husband.

## The real animals
- Domestic cat (*Felis catus*): lives with people almost everywhere in the world. Facts that fit the book: cats sleep a lot (often 12–16 hours a day (check)), love warm sunny spots, purr and rub against people (rubbing leaves their scent and is a friendly greeting), and play by chasing and pouncing, which is practice for hunting. Many cats are curious about shiny, moving things.
- Butterfly (species not named): seen outside the window; part of Whiskers' wish.

## Setting and environment
Indoors, in a cosy house on Maple Street, over one rainy day and evening. Rooms: cupboards and bookshelves, sunny corners, the kitchen with its counter and food bowls, the windowsill where the cats watch the rain. Outside: grey sky, raindrops on the glass, a butterfly, a field the cats remember.

## The value
For a child: even on a grey, boring day there are good things around us: people who love us, a warm home, a fun surprise. Noticing them and saying thank you makes us feel happier.
For a grown-up: gratitude here is about noticing. The cats move from grumbling about what they can't have to appreciating what they do have (care, comfort, each other, a small adventure). The cats show thanks in a cat way: purring and rubbing against Mrs. Maple.
What the book avoids: being told off for being grumpy; gratitude as having to pretend everything is fine.

## Questions to ask
1. Why were the cats grumpy on the rainy day?
2. What changed their mood?
3. How did the cats say thank you to Mrs. Maple without words?
4. What is one special thing that happened today, even if it was a rainy or boring day?
5. Who looks after you? How could you show them thanks?

## Notes
- **Safety:** in the book the cats step close to broken glass. Articles and activities must never suggest children go near or touch broken glass; if a glass breaks, a grown-up cleans it up. Don't make "shiny broken glass" an activity idea.
- The necklace thread is loose in the text (it's not clear whether the cats return it). Don't invent a plot point (e.g. "the cats saved the necklace"); describe it as a surprising, shiny discovery.
- Mittens is "she"; Whiskers and Shadow are "he".
- Cats and real mice: Mittens misses chasing mice outside. For nature/animal articles, it's fine to mention cats are natural hunters; many vets and wildlife groups suggest keeping pet cats indoors to protect wild birds and small animals (check before stating).
