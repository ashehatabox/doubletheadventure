Why do cats purr? If you've read *Rainy Day, Shiny Things* with your child, you've probably heard that question already. Whiskers, Shadow and Mittens are three house cats who spend a grey, rainy day being grumpy, and by the evening they're purring and rubbing against Mrs. Maple, the kind woman who looks after them. Children want to know what that purring means, why cats sleep so much, and why they rub their faces on everything. This article gives you the real facts about domestic cats, shows where each of the book's three cats matches real cat behaviour (and where the story has a little fun), and ends with simple, safe things to share with a 3 to 7 year old.

## Meet the real cat

The heroes of the book are ordinary house cats, the domestic cat, *Felis catus*. It's one of the most familiar animals in the world, and it lives alongside people almost everywhere.

Cats haven't always lived with us, though. Smithsonian Magazine, reporting on a large study of ancient cat DNA, explains that all modern house cats descend from one kind of wild cat, the African wildcat (*Felis silvestris lybica*). Their story with people seems to begin in the Near East, in the region from today's Turkey down to Lebanon. Around 10,000 years ago, farmers there started storing grain. Grain attracted mice, and mice attracted wildcats. Farmers who had fewer mice were probably happy to have cats around, and slowly the two started living together. Cats were also very important in ancient Egypt.

That history explains a lot about the cats on your sofa: they are still built like small hunters. Keep that in mind and almost everything Whiskers, Shadow and Mittens do in the book makes sense.

## Why do cats purr?

Purring is the cat sound children love most, and it's a little mysterious, even to scientists.

### How a cat makes the sound

Dr. Barbara Sherman, who directed the Behavioral Medicine Service at the NC State University College of Veterinary Medicine, explains that purring starts with a signal from the cat's brain and nervous system. That signal makes the muscles of the vocal folds in the cat's voice box (the larynx) vibrate as the cat breathes in and out. The vibrations make tiny bursts of sound every 30 to 40 milliseconds, about 25 times a second, and those bursts blend together into the steady rumble we hear.

For a child: a purr is a cat's voice box going "flutter, flutter, flutter" so fast it sounds like one long hum.

### What purring means

Most of the time, purring goes with good feelings. According to Dr. Sherman, cats are thought to purr to keep in touch with other cats or with people, and purring usually happens during friendly moments: being stroked, greeting a familiar cat, or snuggling with the person who cares for them. It starts very early in life. Mother cats purr while they feed their kittens, and the kittens purr while they nurse.

That's the kind of purr the cats in our book give at dinner time, close to the person who feeds them and tucks them in: a typical "I feel safe with you" moment.

### Purring isn't always "I'm happy"

Here's a surprising fact worth knowing as a parent. The NC State article notes that cats also purr just before falling asleep, and sometimes when they're in pain. Vets examining injured cats sometimes find them purring. So a purr usually means a contented cat, but not always. For children: "A purr often means a cat feels cosy, but we also look at the rest of the cat to see how it's feeling."

## Why do cats sleep so much? Shadow and the sunbeam

Shadow is the quiet one in the book, and his favourite thing is a warm nap in a sunny spot. On the rainy day there's no sun, and he can't settle.

### How many hours a day

Cats really are champion sleepers. Blue Cross, the UK animal welfare charity, says cats sleep for around 15 hours a day on average and can nap for up to 20 hours in a 24-hour period. That's a fact that makes preschoolers giggle: a cat can spend most of its day asleep.

### Why they need so much rest

The reason goes back to that hunting history. Blue Cross explains that cats belong to a family of predators, and hunting uses a lot of energy, so it makes sense for cats to rest a great deal and save energy for when they need it. Pet cats don't have to hunt for their dinner any more, but the instinct and the need for long rests are still there.

Cats are also, as Blue Cross puts it, crepuscular: most active at dawn and dusk. Their wild ancestors hunted at those times, when the light is low and their eyes give them an advantage.

### Why sunny spots?

Lots of cats seek out warm places to nap: a patch of sunshine, a windowsill, a warm lap. A cat who sleeps most of the day will go looking for the cosiest bed in the house, so Shadow missing his sunbeam is true to life.

## Whiskers the explorer: curiosity and those amazing whiskers

The cat named Whiskers is the curious one. He explores every cupboard and shelf, and on the rainy day he wants to be outside chasing a butterfly. His name is a perfect excuse to talk about one of the cat's most amazing tools.

### What whiskers do

PetMD, in an article reviewed by veterinarian Dr. Sandra C. Mitchell, explains that most cats have about 24 whiskers on their cheeks, 12 on each side. Cats also have whiskers above their eyes, on their chin and even on the back of their front legs. Those leg whiskers help them feel movement just below them, which is useful when hunting or climbing.

Whiskers are much more than long hairs. They are so sensitive that they can pick up tiny changes in the movement of the air, so a cat can "feel" things before touching them.

### Can a cat fit through that gap?

Here's a favourite fact. PetMD notes that the whiskers on a cat's cheeks are usually about as wide as the cat's body, which helps the cat judge whether it can squeeze through a gap.

### Whiskers show feelings too

Children can learn to "read" whiskers. According to PetMD, when a cat is relaxed, its whiskers are loose and out to the sides. When it's alert or curious, they point forward. When it's scared or angry, they pull back flat against the face. And one important rule: never trim a cat's whiskers. Cats need them for balance and for finding their way around.

## Mittens the hunter: why cats pounce and play

Mittens is the playful one. She chases yarn, pounces on shadows and, on the rainy day, finds her toy mouse boring.

Cats are natural hunters, and play is how that instinct shows up indoors. The stalk, the wiggle, the sudden pounce on a toy or a moving shadow are the same moves a wildcat uses to catch a mouse. House cats keep doing them all their lives, with toys standing in for prey. A toy mouse lying still is far less interesting than one a grown-up moves around.

### A note about yarn and string

In picture books, cats and balls of yarn go together. In real life, vets are cautious. VCA Animal Hospitals explains that long, thin things such as string, yarn and tinsel can be dangerous if a cat swallows them, because they can get caught in the cat's gut and need surgery. So if your child wants to play "Mittens and the yarn" with a real cat, use a sturdy wand toy with a grown-up holding it, and put string, ribbon and yarn away afterwards.

### Indoor cats and wild birds

Mittens also dreams of chasing mice outside. Real cats are skilled hunters, and that matters for wildlife. Birds Canada says that after habitat loss, cats are the single largest cause of bird deaths in Canada, with free-roaming cats killing an estimated 60 million birds a year. Birds Canada recommends keeping cats inside, or letting them outside only in a controlled way, on a leash or in an enclosed "catio," which it says is best for wild animals and for the cats' own health.

## Why do cats rub against you?

At the end of the rainy day, the three cats show their thanks to Mrs. Maple without any words: they purr and rub against her. Real cats do exactly this, and the reason is lovely.

VCA Animal Hospitals explains that cats have scent glands on their faces: on the cheeks, around the mouth, under the chin and near the ears. When a cat rubs its face on something, those glands leave a scent that we can't smell but other cats can. The affectionate head bump is called **bunting**. Cats rub on furniture and doorways to mark them as part of their home, and when they rub on a person, VCA describes it as a high honour: the cat is marking you as one of its own.

So when the three cats rub against Mrs. Maple, real cat behaviour and the book's message fit neatly: it's a cat's way of saying "you belong to us." If you'd like to explore the gratitude side of the story more, our article on [teaching gratitude to preschoolers](/parents/articles/teaching-gratitude-to-preschoolers-rainy-day-shiny-things) goes into it.

### The slow blink

There's one more cat "language" children can try themselves. Researchers at the University of Sussex and the University of Portsmouth, publishing in the journal *Scientific Reports* in 2020, found that when people narrow their eyes and blink slowly at a cat, cats are more likely to slow blink back, and more likely to come over to a stranger who has slow blinked at them. The scientists describe it as a form of positive communication between cats and people, sometimes called a "cat smile."

## Story or real? How the book bends the cat

*Rainy Day, Shiny Things* is a story, so the cats think and feel the way children do. Here's a quick guide to what's true to life and what's playful.

- **Three cats with different personalities:** true to life. Every cat is an individual.
- **Sleeping in sunny spots and watching rain at the window:** very true. Cats sleep a lot and love warm, cosy places.
- **Purring and rubbing to show love:** true. Purring usually goes with friendly moments, and rubbing shares a cat's scent with the people it trusts.
- **Feeling "grumpy" about the weather and then deciding every day has something special:** that's the story's heart, not cat science. It's a lovely way in to the theme line, "Every day—rainy or sunny—has something special."

One safety note from the story: there's a moment where something breaks in the kitchen. If you talk about it with your child, it's a good chance to say that if a glass ever breaks at home, children (and pets) stay away and a grown-up cleans it up.

## Share it with your child

Pick one or two of these after reading.

- **Purr together.** Ask your child to put a hand on their own throat and hum. That buzzy feeling is a little like how a cat's voice box vibrates when it purrs, about 25 times every second.
- **Whisker measuring tape.** Explain that a cat's whiskers are about as wide as its body. Your child can hold out their arms and pretend they're whiskers, then try to "fit" through a doorway or between two cushions.
- **Read the whiskers.** Show with your fingers: whiskers out to the side means relaxed, pointing forward means curious, pulled back means scared or cross. Then play a guessing game.
- **Try a slow blink.** If you know a calm, friendly cat, your child can try a slow blink from a little distance and see if the cat blinks back.
- **Talk about the dinner scene.** Ask the question from the back of the book: how did the cats say thank you to Mrs. Maple without words? Then ask how your child shows thanks without words: a hug, a drawing, helping.

### Meeting a real cat safely

Cats Protection, the UK cat charity, suggests a few simple rules that work well for young children:

- A grown-up is always there when a child and a cat are together.
- Stroke gently with an open hand and a soft touch.
- Leave the cat alone when it's hiding, sleeping or in its quiet place. Never pull a cat out of a hiding spot.
- A swishing tail, a growl or claws out mean the cat is upset, so step back and give it space.

## Questions parents ask

### Why do cats purr?

Purring usually means a cat feels comfortable and close to someone. It's made by the muscles in the cat's voice box vibrating very fast as it breathes in and out. Cats also purr when they're drowsy and sometimes when they're hurt, so look at the whole cat, not just the sound.

### How many hours a day do cats sleep?

Around 15 hours on average, according to Blue Cross, and up to 20 hours in a day. They're most active around dawn and dusk.

### Why does my cat rub its face on me?

Cats have scent glands on their cheeks, chin and around the mouth. Rubbing, also called bunting, leaves their scent on you. It's a friendly sign that the cat sees you as part of its family.

### Is it safe for cats to play with yarn?

Not on their own. Vets warn that string, yarn and tinsel can be dangerous if swallowed. Use a sturdy wand toy with a grown-up holding it, and put string away after play.

### Should we keep our cat indoors?

Birds Canada recommends keeping cats inside, or outside only on a leash or in an enclosed catio, to protect wild birds and keep cats healthy. Your vet can advise on your own cat.
