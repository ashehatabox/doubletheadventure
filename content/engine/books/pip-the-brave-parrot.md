## Story in short
Pip is a young parrot who lives in the oldest tree of a bright green jungle. His feathers shine and his wings are strong, but his voice is small. He hums when he's alone and stays silent around others, because he remembers being laughed at. One afternoon he hears tiny, frightened cheeps and finds a baby bird that has fallen from its nest, too small to fly back up. Pip tries to lift it with his beak, but it's too heavy. The only way to help is to call out loudly. Heart pounding, he takes a deep breath, spreads his wings and sings louder than ever; his voice shakes, then grows strong, until the jungle hears. The mother bird arrives and together they get the baby home, where the chick sings a tiny copy of Pip's song. From then on Pip sings when he feels happy or brave, and others listen.

## Characters
- **Pip** (parrot, he): young, with shiny feathers, strong wings and a small, quiet voice; shy because he was laughed at; finds courage when someone needs him. The hero. books.js hero line: "a young parrot with a very small voice".
- **The baby bird** (species not named): fallen from its nest, calls for help; later sings Pip's tune.
- **The mother bird** (species not named): answers Pip's call, thanks him with a soft chirp, helps carry the baby home.
- **The other parrots**: loud chirpers in the treetops; once laughed at Pip, later listen to him.
- Look details beyond "shiny feathers" are not in the text (check the illustrations for colour and species).

## The real animals
- Parrot (species not named (check illustrations); a bright green jungle suggests a rainforest parrot such as an Amazon parrot, *Amazona*, or a macaw in Central and South America, or an African grey in central Africa (check)). Facts to check before using: parrots live mostly in warm tropical and subtropical regions; many are very social and noisy and call to keep in touch with their flock; many species nest in tree holes (which fits Pip's home inside the oldest tree); some parrots can copy sounds, which links to the chick copying Pip's song.
- Baby bird: in real life, many young birds found on the ground are fledglings that have left the nest on purpose and are still cared for by their parents (check local wildlife guidance).

## Setting and environment
A bright green jungle (tropical rainforest) with a misty valley and a pink sunrise. Pip lives inside the oldest tree. The key scene is on the forest floor below a high nest, one afternoon.

## The value
For a child: being brave doesn't mean not feeling scared; it means doing the right thing even when your voice shakes. Your voice matters, even if it's quiet.
For a grown-up: courage here is quiet courage: speaking up when it counts. Pip doesn't turn into a loud parrot; he finds his own voice for a reason (someone needs help). The book also shows the effect of being laughed at and how being listened to changes things.
What the book avoids: "shy is bad" or "you have to be loud to be brave"; forcing a quiet child to perform.

## Where the value comes from
Shared across traditions. Related sayings:
- **Ancient China**: "To see what is right and not to do it is want of courage." Original: 見義不為，無勇也。 Source: Analects 2.24 (Wei Zheng), Legge translation. care_flag: none.
- **Japan**: a boy only one inch tall sails to the capital in a rice bowl, with a chopstick oar and a needle for a sword, and bravely drives off an ogre. Original: 一寸法師 (Issun-bōshi). Source: Japanese folktale; Muromachi-period otogizōshi version, softened in Meiji retellings by Iwaya Sazanami (use the common modern version). care_flag: none.
- **Ancient Egypt**: a peasant robbed of his donkeys by a bully pleads for justice before the high steward nine times, ever more eloquently, until his goods are returned (finding your voice). Source: The Eloquent Peasant, Middle Kingdom (c. 1850 BCE); Lichtheim, AEL I (1973); Parkinson (1991, 1997). care_flag: none.

## Key moments
1. Pip wakes in the oldest tree of a misty jungle; he's strong and shiny, but his voice is small.
2. He hums alone but stays silent around others, remembering being laughed at.
3. He hears tiny cries and finds a baby bird fallen from its nest.
4. He tries to lift the baby himself, but it's too heavy.
5. He realises he must call for help, loudly, and is afraid.
6. He sings his loudest song; it wobbles, then grows strong.
7. The mother bird arrives and they bring the baby home; the chick sings Pip's tune back.
8. Pip now sings when he feels happy or brave; others hum along and listen.

## Refrain and theme line
- Refrain: none as a repeated line (Pip's song is the motif).
- Theme line: "Your voice doesn't have to be loud to be strong. It just has to be truly yours."

## Questions to ask
1. Why was Pip quiet when other birds were around?
2. What made Pip decide to sing out loud?
3. How do you think Pip felt when his voice wobbled? Why did he keep going?
4. How do you think it felt for Pip when others laughed? And later when they listened?
5. When is a time it's important to use your voice, even if you feel shy?

## Notes
- **Real-life safety:** the right lesson for children who find a baby bird is to tell a grown-up and not pick it up. Fledglings on the ground are often fine and being fed by their parents. Check guidance from a wildlife or bird-conservation organisation before giving steps. Don't present Pip's lifting the chick as something children should copy.
