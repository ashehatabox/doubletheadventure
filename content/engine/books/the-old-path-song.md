## Story in short
Ama, a young tree pangolin in a green forest in Ghana, loves anything new: new paths, new games, new ways through the trees. Every evening her grandmother Nana taps her tail on a hollow log and sings an old song about stones, a bent palm and a fig tree, and Ama finds it boring. She and her bushbaby friend Kwesi invent paths all over the forest, and it is wonderful. Then the dry, dusty wind arrives, the fruit is gone and the animals grow hungry. None of Ama's new paths leads to food, and when Kwesi asks whether the old ones knew a place, Ama realises she half-remembers Nana's song but never really listened. Instead of trying yet another new path, she chooses to go back. She asks Nana to sing it again and listens to every word; Nana tells her it has passed from grandmother to grandmother. Ama leads the hungry animals along the landmarks in the song, invents a tail-beat on her own scales so they can follow her through the dust, and climbs above the haze to spot a giant fig tree. There is food for everyone. Back home, she turns the old song into a new clapping game.

## Characters
- **Ama** (tree pangolin): brown overlapping scales like a pinecone, pale furry face and tummy, long tail with one golden scale at the tip. Curious, loves novelty; makes the key choices herself.
- **Kwesi** (bushbaby/galago): grey-brown fur, huge round eyes, big ears, bushy tail, white stripe down his nose.
- **Nana** (grandmother pangolin): darker scales with silvery edges, always beside her hollow log; keeps the old song but never solves the problem.
- **Forest animals:** a hornbill with a big curved yellow bill, two duikers (small antelopes), a monkey family.

## The real animals
- **White-bellied (tree) pangolin** (*Phataginus tricuspis*): West and Central African forests including Ghana; climbs with a prehensile tail; eats ants and termites; rolls into a ball. All pangolins are protected internationally (CITES Appendix I) because they are heavily trafficked. (Check details.)
- **Bushbaby / galago** (family Galagidae): small nocturnal primates with very large eyes and ears; great leapers.
- **Hornbills, duikers, monkeys:** forest animals of Ghana; fig trees are "keystone" foods that fruit when little else does (check wording).
- Silk-cotton / kapok tree (*Ceiba pentandra*): giant tree with buttress roots, important in Ghanaian villages.

## Setting and environment
A forest in southern Ghana, from the green season into the dry season with the Harmattan (the dry, dusty north-easterly wind from the Sahara, roughly November to March), ending with the first rain. Landmarks: the old silk-cotton tree and Nana's hollow log, three round stones, a bent old palm, a dry stream bed, a giant fig tree.

## The value
For a child: the old ones know things we still need, and it's never too late to go back and ask. For grown-ups: honouring what came before without rejecting the new; Ama fetches the old wisdom and carries it forward her own way (the beat, the climb, the clapping game). Avoids: grandma saving the day, "new things are bad", shaming (nobody scolds Ama), and a frightening famine.

## Where the value comes from
